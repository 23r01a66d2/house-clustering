Feature Exclusion Rationale: price_per_sqft was evaluated during exploratory data analysis and revealed a strong positive collinear correlation with price (\(r = 0.738\)) and sqft (\(r = 0.782\)). Including it directly in the Euclidean distance space created multicollinearity, heavily biasing the distance metric toward extreme per-sqft luxury outliers and splitting 1.4% of high-end listings into an isolated micro-cluster. Excluding it from clustering distance while retaining it as an observational metric preserved healthy, balanced cluster structures across all five market tiers.

Mathematical Similarity Foundations

1. Log1p Variance Stabilization

Raw rental price (\(\gamma_1 = 4.58\)) and square footage (\(\gamma_1 = 2.01\)) exhibit heavy right-skewed tails. We apply \(\ln(1 + x)\) to compress extreme variance before scaling:

$$x_{\text{trans}} = \ln(1 + x)$$

2. Standardized Similarity Space

Features are centered to zero mean and scaled to unit variance, preventing high-magnitude monetary values from dominating layout counts:

$$z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$$

3. Standardized Euclidean Distance

Multi-attribute proximity between any two properties \(u\) and \(v\) across the {{ n_features }}-dimensional standardized space is computed using Euclidean distance:

$$d(u, v) = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{ {{ n_features }} } (u_i - v_i)^2}$$
K Discovery Rationale: Evaluating \(K \in [2, 10]\) reveals that \(K = 2\) achieves a silhouette peak (\(0.4521\)) due to coarse binary division into low vs high rent. However, \(K = 2\) provides trivial market segmentation. Using the Kneedle elbow detection algorithm on the Within-Cluster Sum of Squares (WCSS) curve identifies \(K = 5\) as the maximum curvature point where incremental variance explained flattens to 12.0%. Fitting \(K = 5\) produces five well-separated, economically meaningful rental archetypes ranging from budget compact living to luxury penthouses.
